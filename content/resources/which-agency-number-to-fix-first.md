**Short answer:** Check delivery margin first. If it is under roughly 50%, nothing downstream (overhead, utilization, cash) will fix it. If delivery margin is healthy, move down the list in the order below. The benchmarks come from two published agency guides; the ordering is our own working heuristic, not something either source prescribes.

Most agency owners already track more numbers than they act on. The harder question is which one deserves attention *first*. If you have read [Profitable but Short on Cash?](/resources/profitable-but-short-on-cash), you know profit and cash can diverge; this guide is about choosing where to look when several numbers look imperfect at once.

## The benchmarks we are using

Parakeeto's agency profitability guide says industry-average net profit is "right around 10%", that 15-20% is what has "traditionally been considered healthy", and that 25-30% is "generally required to cash-flow explosive growth". It also says delivery margin should generally be above 50% agency-wide, and that overhead should not exceed 30% of adjusted gross income (AGI). Source: [parakeeto.com/agency-profitability](https://www.parakeeto.com/blog/measuring-and-improving-your-agencys-profitabilty-the-2023-guide/).

Predictable Profits' guide to agency efficiency metrics gives overlapping figures: "High-performing agencies aim for margins above 50%", "Most agencies aim for a utilization rate between 70% and 85%", and "For most agencies, a project profitability margin of 20-30% is the sweet spot." Source: [predictableprofits.com](https://predictableprofits.com/11-metrics-to-improve-efficiency-in-agencies/).

Two caveats. These are benchmarks from advisory firms, not laws, and your own mix of retainers, projects and freelancers may sit outside them. Also, neither guide ranks the metrics or names a single one to fix first; Predictable Profits only suggests that owners stuck in day-to-day operations start with utilization and efficiency, then move to outcome metrics.

## A simple order of operations

This ordering is our working heuristic. Use it to decide where to look, not as a verdict.

1. **Delivery margin under about 50%?** Start with scoping, pricing and overservicing. Delivery margin is what is left after the direct cost of delivering the work, so a weak number here limits everything else.
2. **Delivery margin fine, net margin under about 10-15%?** Compare overhead with the 30%-of-AGI line.
3. **Margins fine but cash tight?** Look at receivables and payment terms. Profit that has not been collected does not pay payroll.
4. **Everything above looks fine but the team is stretched?** Look at utilization against the 70-85% range, and at capacity for the next quarter.

## A worked example (illustrative numbers)

Suppose a retainer bills $6,000 a month, scoped at 40 hours, and loaded delivery cost is $55 an hour. As scoped, cost is 40 x $55 = $2,200 and delivery margin is about 63%. If the team actually logs 62 hours, cost becomes 62 x $55 = $3,410 and delivery margin falls to about 43%, below the 50% line. The invoice did not change, so a revenue dashboard shows nothing. The problem only shows up when hours logged are compared with hours scoped, client by client. In this example the first number to fix is delivery margin on that client, not overhead or utilization.

## What to do this week

Pick your five largest clients. For each, compare hours scoped with hours logged for the last month and compute delivery margin. If any sit under 50%, that is your first conversation: scope, price or process. Only after that is it worth reviewing overhead, collections and utilization.

This is general operating guidance, not accounting or financial advice. Your accountant or fractional CFO can tell you which benchmarks fit your business.
