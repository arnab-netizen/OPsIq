Many businesses have bought more technology than they have turned into better decisions. PwC's 2026 operations research looks at that gap. This note summarises what it reports, what it does not show, and where OpsIQ's own view begins. Each statement is labelled as **PwC fact**, **OpsIQ interpretation**, **current capability** or **future direction**.

## What PwC reports

**PwC fact.** In PwC's 2026 Digital Trends in Operations Survey (published 23 April 2026), 767 operations and supply-chain leaders at US companies were surveyed. 89% said their technology investments had not fully delivered expected results, and 87% said poor data quality had affected their organization's ability to achieve value from digital initiatives.

**PwC fact.** In PwC's later analysis of the same survey, "How companies can close the AI execution gap in operations" (10 September 2026), integration complexity was the most-cited reason technology investments fell short, at 52% of respondents. PwC also says isolated pilots and fragmented initiatives often prevent companies from turning AI investment into enterprise-wide impact.

## What the research does NOT prove

- The respondents are US operations and supply-chain leaders. The percentages do not describe all businesses, small businesses, owner-led companies or global businesses.
- The survey reports what leaders said. It is not a controlled measurement of what any specific tool did.
- PwC has not reviewed, evaluated or endorsed OpsIQ, and nothing here says it has.

## Technology versus measurable outcome

**PwC fact.** The same analysis says AI success should be measured through operational and financial business outcomes, rather than pilot counts or adoption alone.

**OpsIQ interpretation.** For an owner, "we adopted a tool" and "cash collection improved" are different statements. Only the second is a result. OpsIQ keeps them apart by recording a verified outcome separately from the original recommendation.

## Data and fragmented operations

**PwC fact.** PwC says data quality and reliability are important to scaling successfully, and that connecting workflows end-to-end can improve performance. In "How digital and data leaders are redefining operations" (20 July 2026) it recommends focusing on better data rather than perfect data.

**OpsIQ interpretation.** Owner-led businesses rarely have clean, connected systems. The practical lesson is not to wait for perfect data. It is to be explicit about what the data shows and what is missing.

**Current capability.** Every OpsIQ finding shows the metric and value behind it. When required information is missing, OpsIQ shows the gap instead of hiding it.

## Measuring actual results

**PwC fact.** The same July 2026 article recommends measuring what matters and linking technology investment to operational and financial value. Within a small 4% "leader" cohort that PwC defines in the survey, 83% measure both operational and financial impact of recent digital investments. That cohort is defined by PwC's own maturity conditions and is not a typical business.

**OpsIQ interpretation.** Measurement is a habit before it is a dashboard: decide what should change, then check whether it did.

**Current capability.** OpsIQ ranks findings by severity and urgency, attaches a recommended action with an owner role and timeframe, and lets the owner record the real outcome afterwards. OpsIQ does not detect outcomes automatically.

## What this may mean for owner-led businesses

**OpsIQ interpretation.** The research is about larger operations functions, so any lesson for owner-led businesses is an inference. A reasonable one is that results come from a short chain: trustworthy evidence, a clear priority, an owned action, and a check on what happened. Buying more tools does not by itself supply that chain.
