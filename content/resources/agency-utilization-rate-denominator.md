**Short answer:** Agency utilization rate is billable hours divided by available hours. The part that changes the result is the word "available". Published guides define it in different ways and quote different healthy ranges, so decide your denominator first, write it down, and only then compare your number with a benchmark.

Utilization is one of the first numbers an agency owner is told to watch. It is also easy to misread, because the same team can produce two honest-looking percentages depending on what you count as capacity. This guide is about getting the definition right. If several of your numbers look off at once, start with [Which Agency Number Should You Fix First?](/resources/which-agency-number-to-fix-first) and come back here when utilization is the one that needs attention.

## The formula

The common version is billable hours divided by available hours, multiplied by 100. Agiled's agency utilization guide describes it as billable hours over total available hours, and Everhour's agency calculator gives the same formula. Where they differ is the denominator. Everhour describes a net version in which paid time off, holidays and sick days are removed from the available hours. Parakeeto's discussion of utilization and capacity planning frames it as delivery hours over capacity, and notes that agencies define billable hours and capacity in different ways.

Three denominators are in use:

1. **Gross available hours.** Everyone's contracted hours, with nothing removed.
2. **Net capacity.** Contracted hours minus paid time off, holidays and sick leave.
3. **Role-adjusted capacity.** Net capacity, reduced again for non-delivery duties such as management, sales or internal work.

None of these is wrong. They answer different questions. Gross shows how much of what you pay for turns into billable work. Net shows how busy people are when they are actually at work. Role-adjusted shows how full the delivery roles are.

## An illustrative worked example

These numbers are made up for illustration and are not client data. Suppose a team of 10 people, each contracted for 160 hours in the month, so 1,600 gross hours. Across the team, 160 hours go to paid time off and holidays, leaving 1,440 net hours. The team logs 1,000 billable hours.

- On gross available hours: 1,000 / 1,600 = 62.5%.
- On net capacity: 1,000 / 1,440 = 69.4%.

Same team, same month, same billable hours, and the result moves by almost seven points depending on the denominator. If you compare the first figure with a benchmark built on the second, you may conclude the team is underused when it is not.

## Published ranges do not agree

Agiled says a healthy rate for billable roles is typically 70-85%, and gives role-level ranges: roughly 80-90% for junior staff, 75-85% for mid-level, 60-75% for senior staff, and 30-50% for owners and principals. It also states that 100% is not a goal. Everhour says a good agency target usually falls in a 60-85% range. Parakeeto does not offer one universal benchmark.

So the lower bound of a "healthy" range can be 60% or 70% depending on the source, and a blended team rate hides the fact that senior people and owners are expected to sit lower than junior staff. A single agency-wide number compared with a single benchmark is a weak test.

## What utilization does not tell you

Utilization counts hours. It does not say whether those hours were paid for. A team can log 85% of its available time on work that was never scoped, and that is an overservicing problem rather than a staffing problem. Hours logged on a client but never billed raise utilization and lower delivery margin at the same time. That is why, in our working order of checks, utilization comes after delivery margin, overhead and cash: a high rate does not help if the hours are not turning into revenue. That ordering is our own heuristic, not something these sources prescribe.

## A decision tree for picking your definition

- If your question is "are we paying for more people than we can bill for?", use gross available hours and track it monthly.
- If your question is "are my people overloaded or idle when they are working?", use net capacity.
- If your question is "can this role take another client?", use role-adjusted capacity, and compare each role with its own range rather than a blended one.

Whichever you pick, keep it fixed for at least a quarter. A trend on a consistent definition tells you more than a one-off comparison with someone else's benchmark.

## What to do this week

Pick one definition and write it in one sentence. Compute it for the last three months for each delivery role. Then compare scoped hours with logged hours for your five largest clients, so you know whether the hours behind the percentage are paid for.
