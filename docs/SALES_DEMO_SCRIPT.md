# SALES DEMO SCRIPT

**Document:** Exact demo flow for sales team  
**Duration:** 15-20 minutes (interactive)  
**Audience:** Sales team, prospects, decision makers  
**Updated:** 2026-05-22  

---

## OPENING (2 minutes)

**Confidence, not pitch:**

> "Great to meet you. Before I dive into the demo, I want to ask — tell me about the biggest frustration you have right now. Is it tracking who's doing what? Proving impact to leadership? Something else?"

**Listen for their answer.** We'll use it later to close.

**Then:**

> "I ask because we built OpsIQ specifically for this. But before I show you, let me ask one more quick thing: How are you currently tracking [their problem]?"

**Listen.** Get them to say "spreadsheets" or "Slack" or "nothing."

---

## THE DEMO (15 minutes)

### Section 1: Login & Workspace View (1 minute)

**Action: Show login screen**

> "This is the login. Simple — email and password. No complex setup. Once you log in, this is what you see."

**Action: Show workspace dashboard**

> "This is your workspace. Think of this as your home base. From here, you manage everything — engagements, your team, metrics. One place instead of 10 different tools."

**Key message: Single source of truth.**

---

### Section 2: Create an Engagement (2 minutes)

**Scenario: "Let's say you're consulting with a mid-market company that needs to improve their operations. Here's how you start."**

**Action: Click "New Engagement"**

> "You click 'New Engagement.' Give it a name, describe the scope, set the timeline."

**Action: Fill in form**
- Name: "ABC Corp - Operations Transformation"
- Scope: "Reduce operational costs by 15%"
- Timeline: "12 weeks"
- Business Condition: "Revenue flat, operations inefficient"
- Intervention Mode: "Structured transformation"

> "You can see — you're capturing the business context from day one. Not just 'we're doing consulting,' but WHY and what success looks like."

**Action: Show engagement created**

> "Boom — engagement created. Now, the magic starts here."

**Key message: Context matters.**

---

### Section 3: Add Blockers & Interventions (3 minutes)

**Scenario: "Week 1, you identify the first blocker."**

**Action: Click "Add Blocker"**

> "You find out: Their finance team doesn't have visibility into departmental spend. That's blocking progress."

**Action: Create blocker**
- Title: "Finance team lacks departmental spend visibility"
- Severity: "Critical"
- Assigned to: "CFO"
- Due date: "Week 3"

> "You assign it to the CFO — they own this. Set a deadline. Now the CFO sees it in their dashboard. No email lost in the noise. Accountability baked in."

**Action: Add action under blocker**

> "Now, you break this into actions. The first action: 'Implement cost center tracking in finance system.'"

**Action: Create action**
- Description: "Implement cost center tracking"
- Owner: "Finance Manager"
- Timeline: "Week 2"

> "Finance Manager now owns this action. They'll see it on their dashboard. When they complete it, they check it off."

**Action: Show action status change**

> "When it's done, it moves to 'complete.' Now the blocker is one step closer to resolved. And here's the key — you can see exactly who did what, when."

**Action: Add a second action (rapid)**

> "Let's add another action: 'Train managers on new spend tracking.' Assigned to HR. Due week 3."

> "Now you have a flow — blockers have actions, actions have owners. Everything connected. Everything visible."

**Key message: Clear ownership + accountability = action.**

---

### Section 4: Dashboard & Metrics (2 minutes)

**Scenario: "It's now week 4. Let's check progress."**

**Action: Go to Engagement Overview**

> "Here's your engagement dashboard. You can see:"

**Action: Point to each metric**

- **Blockers:** 5 total, 2 resolved, 3 in progress, 0 critical
- **Actions:** 12 total, 8 done, 4 in flight, 1 overdue
- **Team:** 8 people involved, engagement, contribution
- **Health:** Green (on track), timeline 23% complete

> "This is what you show your sponsor in the weekly standup. It's one screen. They see health, progress, risks. No spreadsheet. No manual updates."

**Action: Click "Overdue Actions"**

> "You see that one action is overdue — 'Stakeholder training.' It was due yesterday, assigned to HR. Now you can follow up. You have context. You know why it matters. You can help."

**Action: Show team view**

> "Here's your team. Everyone on this engagement. You can see who's most active, who hasn't logged in. You can see contribution. Are people really engaged, or are you doing all the work? This tells you."

**Key message: Visibility into execution reality.**

---

### Section 5: Governance & Decision Support (2 minutes)

**Scenario: "It's week 6. Time to make a go/no-go decision. Do we stay on track or pivot?"**

**Action: Go to Evidence/Decision Support Section**

> "As you work through the engagement, you collect evidence. This is evidence of business conditions, results, blockers, outcomes."

**Action: Show evidence types**
- KPI snapshot (revenue, cost, headcount)
- Blocker status (resolved? still active?)
- Stakeholder feedback (sentiment, quotes)
- Lessons learned (what's working, what's not)

> "On week 6, you review all this evidence and make a decision: Do we continue as planned, or do we change course?"

**Action: Show decision gate**

> "You record the decision, who made it, why. You attach evidence. Fast forward to next year — you have a complete record. Why did we succeed? Why did we fail? You have the answer."

**Key message: Decisions are documented, not forgotten.**

---

### Section 6: Reporting & Impact (2 minutes)

**Scenario: "Engagement is done. 12 weeks later. Time to show impact."**

**Action: Go to Report / Impact View**

> "You built these features:"

**Action: Show outcomes**
- Finance system tracking implemented ✓
- Department spend visibility enabled ✓
- Manager training completed ✓
- Stakeholder satisfaction: 8/10

> "And the business result?"

**Action: Show impact metrics**
- Operational cost reduction: 18% (target was 15%)
- Decision velocity: 40% faster
- Team engagement: Up from 5/10 to 8/10

> "You can put this in front of the CFO. Show impact. Show ROI. It's not a guess. It's documented."

**Key message: Impact is measurable.**

---

### Section 7: Team Collaboration (1 minute)

**Scenario: "But it's not just for you, the consultant. Your whole team sees this."**

**Action: Show team member view**

> "Let's look at the view from a team member's perspective. They log in, see their dashboard — their actions, their blockers, their progress."

**Action: Show action detail**

> "They click on an action, see the context, the discussion, any files attached. They check it off when done. No Slack threads. No email. One place."

**Action: Show comment/discussion**

> "Here's a discussion thread on a blocker — team members collaborating, sharing ideas, working through problems. Everything stays with the work. Not scattered."

**Key message: Team alignment without chaos.**

---

## CLOSING QUESTIONS (2 minutes)

**Don't pitch. Ask.**

> "So, thinking about [their problem they mentioned at the start] — does this look like something that would help?"

**If yes:** 

> "Great. What would be most important to test first? And how would we know if this was working for you?"

**If hesitation:**

> "What's the concern? Is it the feature set, the price, the timing, something else?"

**Listen.**

## COMMON OBJECTIONS & RESPONSES

### "It looks like project management software."

> "I get that. It can track projects. But it's different — it's designed for governance and decision-making. You're not just tracking tasks; you're documenting the business condition, the intervention, the decisions made, and the results. A PM tool doesn't do that. Think of it as consulting software, not project software."

### "We use Asana. Why would we need this?"

> "Asana is great for tasks. OpsIQ is for outcomes. Use Asana for your sprints, your tasks, your timelines. Use OpsIQ for the business logic — what's the problem, what decisions did we make, what was the impact? They complement each other."

### "How much customization can we do?"

> "Right now — the core workflows are fixed. We don't build custom features during pilot. But we listen to every customer. If you ask for something and others ask too, we build it. Our philosophy: Build once for everyone, not custom for one."

### "Can we integrate with Salesforce / HubSpot / etc?"

> "Not yet. That's on our roadmap. For now, you'd use OpsIQ standalone or manually sync some data. If Salesforce integration is critical, we should talk about timing."

### "What about security / compliance?"

> "We have industry-standard security — SSL encryption, encrypted at rest, secure password storage. We're not SOC 2 certified yet. If you need HIPAA, FedRAMP, or PCI compliance, we're not ready. We're planning compliance for 2027."

### "Will my data be safe?"

> "Yes. We use AWS, encrypted storage, regular backups. If we shut down, you can export your data (CSV, JSON). If you cancel, we delete it after 30 days. We're not a fly-by-night startup — our investors and team are committed for the long term."

## CLOSING THE DEMO

**If interested:**

> "Great. Here's what's next. We do pilots — 6-month engagement, $5,000/year. We assign a customer success person, you get weekly check-ins, direct access to the product team. You're not just a customer; you're part of building this. Does that interest you?"

**If not sure:**

> "Totally fair. Why don't I send you a 1-pager on how [Competitor X] uses us, and we can grab coffee next week? No pressure."

**If "no":**

> "No problem. But I'm curious — what would need to be different for this to work for you? Timeline? Feature? Price?"

**Always end with:**

> "Thanks for your time. We genuinely appreciate the feedback. If things change, you know where to find us."

---

**End of Script**

**Demo tips:**
- Don't talk the whole time. Let them ask questions.
- Use their language. If they say "blocks," say "blocks." If they say "risks," say "risks."
- Show one workflow completely (blocker → action → complete) before jumping around.
- Pause after key features. Let that sink in.
- Ask "Does that make sense?" frequently. Don't assume.
- If they ask about a feature not in the demo, note it. Don't try to fake it.
